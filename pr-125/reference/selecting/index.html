    Selecting¶

    A selection is the Selection that select(record, **labels) returns: a record at the labels named. select reads the labels as xarray's sel does. One label squeezes its dim out of the selection, and a list, a slice or a partial date string keeps it.

    The call¶

    from datarecord import select

view = select(record, snapshot=1)
view.labels

    {'snapshot': 1}

    select takes any RecordLike, a framework's own object included, and record.select(**labels) calls it on a Record. A Selection is itself a RecordLike, with three more members:

    Member Value
    base the record selected from, never a Selection
    labels the keywords asked for, normalised: one label for a squeezed dim, else a list of labels
    schema the base's schema without the squeezed dims and what refers to them

    labels has what you asked for, and the labels that a selection drops through references show only in view.dims. view.select(**labels) merges its keywords into labels, a later keyword replacing one of the same name, and selects base once.

    Keywords¶

    A keyword is the name of one of three things:

    • A dim, such as snapshot=1 or bus=["north"].
    • A coordinate of a multi-dim that is no dim, such as from= of a corridor, which narrows the multi-dim's tuples.
    • A property by its plain name, which narrows its subject to the labels whose value, its default applied, is selected. A label whose value is NULL goes.

    A coordinate or property keyword never squeezes, so labels has its value as a list. A tuple is selected through its coordinates, never by the multi-dim's own name.

    Values¶

    Value Selects The dim
    one label that label, as pandas' .loc matches it squeezed
    a partial date string, on a datetime axis every label in its span kept, even at one
    a list each label exactly, read as the dim's dtype kept, even at one
    slice(start, stop) the labels between, both ends included, None open kept, even at one

    A date string is partial where it is coarser than the axis, so "2013-02" on a daily axis selects February and "2013-02-01" selects one label. In a list, a date string is one instant. A slice takes an axis of numbers or datetimes and compares its ends with the labels sorted, so snapshot=slice(1, None) gives [1, 2]. The labels a selection keeps are in axis order.

    select raises ValueError at the call, naming the keyword, for:

    • a name that the schema declares as no dim, coordinate or property, or as a multi-dim
    • a property name that several subjects declare, or a coordinate name that several multi-dims declare
    • a label the axis lacks, or an empty list
    • a slice with a step, of an axis of text, with an end the axis cannot compare, or covering no label
    • a squeezed label that another keyword's selection drops
    • squeezing every dim of a record that declares attributes, because attribute data over no dim is no record
    • a squeeze that would change a default, as Squeezing describes

    Squeezing¶

    To squeeze is to select one label of a dim, which drops the dim from the selection's schema. A list of one keeps the dim. Squeezing snapshot at 1 keeps the rows at 1 and the rows NULL on snapshot, settles them along snapshot alone, and drops the column:

    wind has the value it had at snapshot 1, and the NULL row stays NULL on entity.

    Squeezing a dim d at a label l changes the schema and the rows:

    • d leaves the schema with its axis and its properties, and so does every property that refers to d.
    • A default_by keyed on a property that refers to d applies its entry for l as the default. A default_by keyed on a property of d applies the entry for the category that l has.
    • A multi-dim that draws on d loses that coordinate. One left with no coordinate is squeezed too.
    • An attribute over squeezed dims alone becomes one value over no dim.
    • partial and every Flags lose d.

    record.select(ctype="Generator") squeezes ctype, so entity.ctype leaves the schema and the default of p_max_pu is 1.0, the Generator entry being absent. Where a default_by lists l and a kept label is NULL on the property, select refuses the squeeze. That label would take the default for l in the selection and not in the base. A list of one, such as ctype=["Load"], keeps the property and every default.

    What goes with a dropped label¶

    A selection keeps every axis closed under reference, as removing a label does (layers):

    • A label whose property, its default applied, is a dropped label goes. A NULL is not a label and stays.
    • A tuple that contains a dropped label goes, and its other labels stay.
    • An attribute row that has a dropped label, in its column or in a field of a struct, goes. A row NULL there stays and applies to the kept labels.
    • This repeats until nothing more goes.

    select(bus=["north"]) drops the bus south and the two connections that contain it.

    Nothing flows the other way. A dim keeps a label that no kept label refers to, such as a carrier that no entity has. record.select(ctype="Generator") drops dc through entity.ctype and keeps the bus south.

    Laziness and layers¶

    select reads the axes and properties it needs when you call it, and every attribute stays a plan on the base's connection. A selection needs neither the data each revision adds (its layers) nor partial. view.flags() returns the base's flags without the squeezed dims, which overstate the selection's rows.

    A selection writes nothing, and WorkingRecord refuses one. Over a WorkingRecord, a narrowed axis keeps the labels it had at the call, and an attribute reads later edits at those labels.

    lift¶

    To lift is to put the squeezed labels back on rows written over a selection, with Selection.lift. view.lift(attribute, rel) adds a column at its label for each squeezed dim of the attribute, and puts a squeezed coordinate back into its struct:

    The rows of p_max_pu over select(record, snapshot=1) come back with snapshot at 1.

    lift reads only labels and the base's schema. It returns an attribute that the base declares over no squeezed dim unchanged, so lift("p_nom", rel) returns rel. The coordinates come back in the base's column order. A row NULL on the squeezed dim in the base was settled to the label, so it comes back at the label. lift raises KeyError when the base declares no such attribute.
